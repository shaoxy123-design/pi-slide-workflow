## Slide 1 — Why adjust at month-end?

BrightTutors prepares its accounts on **May 31**. Under accrual accounting, May's expenses belong in May even when cash is paid earlier or later.

Two situations need attention:

- Rent was paid in advance: some of that benefit has now been used.
- Employees have earned wages: the business has not paid them yet.

The purpose is to recognize the expense for May and update the related asset or liability. These two adjusting entries do not change cash.

**Grouped visual idea:** A May calendar with “Paid earlier” and “Paid later” paths leading to “May expense,” as one native group. Keep body text separate.

## Slide 2 — Rent paid before it is used

On **May 1**, BrightTutors pays **$1,200** for **May and June rent**, with **$600 allocated to each month**.

| Entry | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| May 1: initial payment | Prepaid Rent $1,200 | Cash $1,200 |
| May 31: adjusting entry | Rent Expense $600 | Prepaid Rent $600 |

By May 31, May's rent has been used. Recognize **$600 rent expense** and reduce the prepaid asset by **$600**. **Prepaid Rent remaining: $600**, for June.

**Grouped visual idea:** A native rent strip: “May: $600 used” and “June: $600 remaining.” Group shapes and labels; keep the table native.

## Slide 3 — Wages earned before they are paid

Employees earned **$300** during May. These wages are **unpaid and unrecorded before adjustment** at **May 31**.

| Entry | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| May 31: adjusting entry | Wages Expense $300 | Wages Payable $300 |

Recognize **$300 wages expense** for May and **$300 wages payable**. Cash will be paid later; it is not part of this adjusting entry.

**Grouped visual idea:** A native process diagram: “May work completed” → “$300 owed at May 31” → “Cash paid later.” Group all shapes and labels.

## Slide 4 — What changes after both adjustments?

Compare with the **unadjusted balances after recording the May 1 rent payment**. Show the effects of these two adjustments only; complete May balances are outside this example. **Ignore tax.**

| Item | Combined change |
|---|---|
| Profit | Decreases by $900: $600 rent + $300 wages |
| Assets | Decrease by $600: Prepaid Rent |
| Liabilities | Increase by $300: Wages Payable |
| Cash | No change from these two adjustments |

**Grouped visual idea:** One native group of change cards: profit −$900, assets −$600, liabilities +$300, and “Cash unchanged.” Keep the table editable.

## Slide 5 — Three quick checks

1. How much Prepaid Rent remains after the May 31 adjustment?
2. Which account is credited for the unpaid May wages, and by how much?
3. Do these two adjusting entries change cash? What is their combined effect on profit?

**Answers for editable speaker notes:**

1. **$600**, representing June rent.
2. **Wages Payable, $300**.
3. **Cash is unchanged. Profit decreases by $900**, because May expenses increase by $600 + $300.
